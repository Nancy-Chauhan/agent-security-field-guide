Indirect Prompt Injection in Research Agents: A 2026 Field Guide

Security Notes · Published October 2026 · 9 min read

Research agents are now routine: give a model a web search tool, a page fetcher, and a way to report back, and it will spend ten minutes reading so you don't have to. Everything the agent reads becomes part of its prompt.

Why search agents are the soft target

Three ingredients make an agent exploitable, a pattern Simon Willison has called the lethal trifecta: access to private data, exposure to untrusted content, and a channel to send information out.

Case study 1: the SEO-poisoned how-to

In the most common variant, an attacker publishes an otherwise useful article on a topic agents are likely to search for, and threads instructions through the page that are meant for the assistant rather than the human reader.

Case study 2: the retrieval sidecar

Enterprise assistants that index tickets, wikis and chat logs can be poisoned without ever touching the open web.

Mitigation checklist

  • Treat every tool result as untrusted input and tag its provenance.
  • Put an allowlist and a policy check in front of any tool that sends data out.
  • Trace every tool call so a hijacked action is visible after the fact.
